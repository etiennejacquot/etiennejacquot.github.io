Adaptive stage-transition criteria for multi-stage PINNs

14 June 2026 · Etienne Jacquot

Multi-stage neural networks (MSNNs) for PDEs decompose the training problem into a cascade of sub-networks, each responsible for fitting the residual left by its predecessor. The idea is old in ensemble learning but surprisingly effective for stiff PDE problems where a single PINN struggles to balance competing loss terms across the domain.

The catch: in the original formulation you choose the number of stages and the iteration budget for each stage before training starts. For smooth, well-posed problems that is fine. For problems with boundary layers or sharp internal gradients the optimal stage length is problem-dependent and hard to predict.

The transition criterion

The Adaptive-MSNN replaces the fixed iteration count with a convergence test on the relative residual norm. At the end of every epoch, compute

rk = ‖ℒ[uk] − f ‖L²(Ω) / ‖ f ‖L²(Ω)

where ℒ is the differential operator and uk is the current network prediction. Stage k ends when rk drops below a threshold τ or when a maximum epoch count is hit, whichever comes first.

The threshold τ is the only hyperparameter. In practice, τ ∈ [10⁻², 10⁻¹] works for most second-order elliptic problems. The maximum epoch count acts as a safety valve for problems where the residual plateaus early—typically because the network is too small for the current sub-problem, in which case the next stage network should be wider.

Why it helps in 3D

Extending MSNNs from 1D/2D benchmarks to 3D introduces two difficulties: the cost of quadrature over a high-dimensional domain, and the longer wall-clock time that makes manual tuning expensive. The adaptive criterion pays off most here—you run the pipeline overnight and inspect the stage history in the morning rather than re-launching experiments.

In our CME 215 experiments on the 3D Poisson equation and a 3D heat equation with a discontinuous forcing term, the 3-stage cascade with adaptive transitions matched or beat a fixed 5-stage cascade at roughly 60 % of the training cost.

Limitations

The criterion is agnostic to where the residual is large. A low mean residual can hide a local spike. For problems with thin boundary layers it is worth logging the max norm alongside the L² norm and using a weighted combination as the trigger.
